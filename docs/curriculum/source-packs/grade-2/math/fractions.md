# Arizen Curriculum Factory v1

## Grade 2 Mathematics Source Pack

## Unit: Fractions

### 1. Unit Purpose

This unit introduces Grade 2 learners to simple fractions using concrete objects, visual models, and everyday sharing situations.

The unit focuses on:

* Understanding a half as 1 out of 2 equal parts
* Understanding a quarter as 1 out of 4 equal parts
* Identifying halves and quarters in shapes, objects, and daily life
* Comparing 1/2 and 1/4 using the same whole
* Using fractions in simple real-life situations such as sharing food
* Recognizing simple fraction patterns
* Explaining fraction ideas using words, drawings, and objects

The unit should avoid abstract fraction rules too early. Learners should first see, cut, fold, shade, share, and compare.

### 3. Big Ideas

A learner should understand these ideas by the end of the unit:

1. A fraction is part of a whole.

2. A whole can be divided into equal parts.

3. A half means one of two equal parts.

4. A quarter means one of four equal parts.

5. The parts must be equal. If the parts are not equal, they are not proper halves or quarters.

6. When the same whole is used, 1/2 is bigger than 1/4.

7. Fractions are used in real life when people share food, divide materials, measure, cook, or arrange objects.

8. Fractions can be shown using objects, drawings, folded paper, shaded shapes, and number patterns.

### 5. Common Misconceptions

The lesson generator must watch for these misconceptions:

1. **Any two parts are halves**

Wrong idea: If a shape is cut into two parts, each part is automatically a half.

Correction: The two parts must be equal.

2. **Any four parts are quarters**

Wrong idea: If a shape is cut into four parts, each part is automatically a quarter.

Correction: The four parts must be equal.

3. **1/4 is bigger than 1/2 because 4 is bigger than 2**

Correction: When the whole is the same size, a half is bigger because the whole is divided into fewer equal parts.

4. **Fractions only happen in shapes**

Correction: Fractions happen in food, paper, time, groups, games, and sharing.

5. **Different wholes can be compared carelessly**

Wrong idea: A quarter of a large cake may look bigger than half of a small biscuit.

Correction: To compare 1/2 and 1/4 fairly, use the same whole.

6. **Shaded part alone defines the fraction**

Correction: The learner must look at both the shaded part and the total equal parts.

### 7. Unit Lesson Sequence

The unit should follow this progression:

#### Lesson 1: Introduction to Halves Using Circular Cut-outs

Concept focus:
A circle can be divided into 2 equal parts. Each equal part is one half.

Learner should be able to:

* fold or divide a circle into two equal parts
* identify one half of a circular shape
* explain that halves must be equal

Concrete material:

* circular paper
* chapati drawing
* orange slice drawing

---

#### Lesson 2: Introduction to Halves Using Rectangular Cut-outs

Concept focus:
A rectangle can be divided into 2 equal parts in different ways.

Learner should be able to:

* fold or divide a rectangle into two equal parts
* identify one half of a rectangular shape
* understand that halves can look different but must be equal

Concrete material:

* rectangular paper
* book cover
* slice of bread
* manila paper

---

#### Lesson 3: Identifying 1/2 in Everyday Objects

Concept focus:
A half can be seen in food, paper, drawings, and shared objects.

Learner should be able to:

* identify halves in daily objects
* tell whether a shown part is half or not half
* explain why the parts must be equal

Concrete material:

* bread
* banana
* paper
* chapati
* picture cards

---

#### Lesson 4: Introduction to Quarters Using Circular Cut-outs

Concept focus:
A circle can be divided into 4 equal parts. Each equal part is one quarter.

Learner should be able to:

* fold or divide a circle into four equal parts
* identify one quarter of a circular shape
* explain that quarters must be equal

Concrete material:

* circular paper
* chapati drawing
* orange drawing

---

#### Lesson 5: Introduction to Quarters Using Rectangular Cut-outs

Concept focus:
A rectangle can be divided into 4 equal parts.

Learner should be able to:

* fold or divide a rectangle into four equal parts
* identify one quarter of a rectangular shape
* understand that quarters can be arranged in different ways

Concrete material:

* rectangular paper
* bread slice
* manila paper
* exercise book drawing

---

#### Lesson 6: Identifying 1/4 in Everyday Objects

Concept focus:
A quarter is one out of four equal parts.

Learner should be able to:

* identify quarters in everyday situations
* tell whether a part is one quarter or not
* explain why the four parts must be equal

Concrete material:

* food sharing examples
* paper folds
* shaded shapes
* picture cards

---

#### Lesson 7: Comparing Fractions: 1/2 and 1/4

Concept focus:
When the whole is the same size, 1/2 is bigger than 1/4.

Learner should be able to:

* compare one half and one quarter using the same whole
* explain why 1/2 is bigger than 1/4
* avoid thinking that 1/4 is bigger because 4 is bigger than 2

Concrete material:

* same-size circles
* same-size rectangles
* chapati drawings

---

#### Lesson 8: Fractions in Daily Life: Sharing Food

Concept focus:
Fractions are useful when sharing food fairly.

Learner should be able to:

* use halves and quarters in simple sharing situations
* identify fair and unfair sharing
* explain how to share one item among 2 or 4 people

Concrete material:

* chapati
* mandazi
* bread
* fruit
* snack drawings

---

#### Lesson 9: Making Patterns with Fractions

Concept focus:
Fractions can appear in simple visual patterns.

Learner should be able to:

* recognize simple patterns using halves and quarters
* complete a pattern with shaded fraction shapes
* describe the pattern using words

Example patterns:

* half, quarter, half, quarter
* shaded half, unshaded half, shaded half
* circle half, rectangle half, circle half

---

#### Lesson 10: Digital Games with Fractions

Concept focus:
Learners can practise identifying halves and quarters through games.

Learner should be able to:

* choose shapes showing halves or quarters
* sort examples and non-examples
* match fraction symbols to pictures

Game activity types:

* tap the half
* tap the quarter
* drag picture to correct label
* choose the fair sharing picture
* match `1/2` to half
* match `1/4` to quarter

---

#### Lesson 11: Fractions: Practice and Application

Concept focus:
Learners apply halves and quarters across different representations.

Learner should be able to:

* identify halves and quarters in circles and rectangles
* shade halves and quarters
* compare 1/2 and 1/4
* solve simple sharing problems

This lesson should not introduce a new concept. It should consolidate the unit.

---

#### Lesson 12: Fractions: Assessment and Reflection

Concept focus:
Learners demonstrate understanding of halves, quarters, comparison, and real-life use.

Learner should be able to:

* answer mixed questions on halves and quarters
* identify examples and non-examples
* explain one real-life use of fractions
* reflect on what they learned

This lesson should include assessment-style tasks, not generic teaching content.

### 8. Journey Step Standards

Every Fractions lesson must follow the 10-step Arizen journey.

#### Step 1: Welcome

Purpose:
Invite the learner into the lesson warmly.

Good format:
"Today we are going to explore [concept] using [object/context]."

Example:
"Today we are going to learn about halves by folding a circle into two equal parts."

Avoid:

* generic praise with no topic
* vague phrases like "Let us learn more about this topic"

---

#### Step 2: Mission

Purpose:
State what the learner will be able to do.

Good format:
"By the end of this lesson, you will be able to..."

Example:
"By the end of this lesson, you will be able to show one half of a circle and explain why the two parts must be equal."

Avoid:

* overly broad outcomes
* outcomes unrelated to the title

---

#### Step 3: Think First

Purpose:
Activate prior knowledge.

Good question types:

* "Have you ever shared a chapati with someone?"
* "What happens when you fold paper into two equal parts?"
* "Which piece looks fair?"

Good Think First tasks:

* choose between fair and unfair sharing
* look at a simple shape and predict
* connect to food sharing

Avoid:

* questions that require formal fraction knowledge before teaching
* generic questions like "What do you know about fractions?"

---

#### Step 4: Learn

Purpose:
Teach the main idea clearly.

A good Learn step must include:

* simple definition
* concrete object
* visualizable explanation
* key rule
* common mistake warning if useful

Example structure:

1. Start with object
2. Divide into equal parts
3. Name the fraction
4. Explain the symbol
5. Give one non-example

Example:
"A half means one of two equal parts. Imagine a chapati cut into two pieces of the same size. Each piece is one half. If one piece is big and the other is small, the pieces are not halves because they are not equal."

Avoid:

* placeholder content
* one-sentence definitions only
* abstract symbols without objects

---

#### Step 5: Connect

Purpose:
Link the concept to real life.

Good contexts:

* sharing food
* folding paper
* cutting fruit
* dividing class materials
* drawing and shading

Example:
"We use halves when we share one mandazi fairly between two people. Each person gets one equal part."

Avoid:

* nonsensical text like "Where do you see assessment and reflection at home?"
* repeating the lesson title without meaning

---

#### Step 6: Example

Purpose:
Show a worked example.

A good Example step must include:

* a clear problem
* a concrete situation
* step-by-step explanation
* final answer

Example:
"Example: Amina folds a rectangular paper into two equal parts. She shades one part. How much has she shaded?

Step 1: The paper is one whole.
Step 2: It is divided into 2 equal parts.
Step 3: One part is shaded.
Answer: Amina shaded one half, or 1/2."

Avoid:

* "Let me show you" with no actual example
* examples that only repeat the definition
* examples without an answer

---

#### Step 7: Practice

Purpose:
Let the learner apply the idea.

A good Practice step must have at least 3 tasks.

Practice progression:

1. concrete/object task
2. visual identification task
3. application or explanation task

Example practice set:

1. Fold a paper circle into two equal parts. Point to one half.
2. Look at three shapes. Choose the one that shows 1/2.
3. Draw a rectangle and shade one half.

Avoid:

* only one task
* vague instruction like "Practice fractions"
* tasks unrelated to the lesson concept

---

#### Step 8: Quick Check

Purpose:
Check one important understanding.

A good Quick Check must include:

* one clear question
* 3 or 4 answer options
* one correct answer
* plausible wrong answers
* correctIndex set correctly

Example:
Question:
Which shape shows one half?

Options:
A. A circle divided into 2 equal parts with 1 part shaded
B. A circle divided into 3 parts with 1 part shaded
C. A circle divided into 4 equal parts with 1 part shaded

Correct answer:
A

Avoid:

* definition copied word-for-word from Learn
* missing correct answer
* trick questions
* multiple correct answers

---

#### Step 9: Reflect

Purpose:
Help the learner think about what they understood.

Good reflection prompts:

* "How can you tell if two parts are equal?"
* "Where have you seen halves at home?"
* "What is one difference between a half and a quarter?"
* "What mistake should you avoid when finding quarters?"

Avoid:

* generic prompts like "What did you learn today?"
* prompts unrelated to the concept
* prompts that are too abstract

---

#### Step 10: Complete

Purpose:
Close the lesson with a clear sense of achievement.

Good format:
"You can now [specific skill]."

Example:
"Great work. You can now identify one half of a circle and explain that halves must be equal."

Avoid:

* generic reward text only
* claims the learner mastered skills not taught in the lesson

---

### 10. Quality Rubric

Each lesson should be scored out of 100.

#### Outcome Alignment — 15 points

15: All steps match the lesson outcome
10: Mostly aligned, minor drift
5: Some steps are generic or off-topic
0: Lesson title and content do not match

#### Learn Quality — 15 points

15: Clear, concrete, age-appropriate explanation
10: Understandable but thin
5: Too brief or generic
0: Missing, placeholder, or incorrect

#### Example Quality — 15 points

15: Worked example with steps and answer
10: Example is useful but too short
5: Example exists but weak
0: Missing, empty, or placeholder

#### Practice Quality — 20 points

20: 3+ meaningful tasks with progression
15: 2-3 tasks but limited variety
10: One useful task only
5: Vague or weak practice
0: Missing or placeholder

#### Quick Check Quality — 15 points

15: Valid MCQ, correct answer, good distractors
10: Valid but too easy
5: Question exists but weak
0: Missing correct answer, invalid, or copied directly from Learn

#### Real-Life Relevance — 10 points

10: Strong everyday context
7: Some real-life connection
3: Generic connection
0: No real-life connection or nonsensical context

#### Progression Fit — 5 points

5: Fits unit sequence well
3: Somewhat connected
0: Does not build from previous lesson or duplicates another lesson

#### Reflection Quality — 5 points

5: Meaningful and concept-specific
3: Generic but usable
0: Missing or unrelated

Passing threshold:

* 85-100: Student-ready
* 70-84: Needs repair
* Below 70: Rebuild

Automatic fail conditions:

* Placeholder text
* Missing Learn step
* Missing Example step
* Practice has fewer than 2 meaningful tasks
* Quick Check has no correct answer
* Content copied from another lesson
* Lesson title and content do not match

### 12. Content Bank

#### Halves Examples

* A chapati cut into 2 equal pieces
* A paper circle folded into 2 equal parts
* A rectangle divided into 2 equal parts
* A slice of bread cut into 2 equal pieces
* A banana shared equally between 2 children

#### Quarters Examples

* A chapati cut into 4 equal pieces
* A rectangle divided into 4 equal parts
* A paper circle folded twice to make 4 equal parts
* An orange shared among 4 children
* A square divided into 4 equal parts

#### Comparing 1/2 and 1/4 Examples

* Same-size chapati cut into halves and quarters
* Same-size rectangle shaded 1/2 and 1/4
* Same-size paper circles folded differently
* Asking which piece gives more food when the whole is the same

#### Non-Examples

* A circle cut into 2 unequal parts is not showing halves
* A rectangle cut into 4 unequal parts is not showing quarters
* A very large quarter cannot be compared fairly with a very small half unless the wholes are the same size

### 13. Recommended Practice Task Types

Use these task types across the unit:

1. Fold and point
   "Fold a paper into equal parts and point to one part."

2. Shade
   "Draw a rectangle and shade one half."

3. Identify
   "Choose the shape that shows one quarter."

4. Explain
   "Tell why this shape does not show halves."

5. Compare
   "Which is bigger, 1/2 or 1/4 of the same chapati?"

6. Match
   "Match the picture to 1/2 or 1/4."

7. Sort
   "Sort the pictures into halves, quarters, and not equal parts."

8. Real-life sharing
   "One orange is shared equally among 4 children. What fraction does each child get?"

### 14. Recommended Quick Check Patterns

#### Pattern A: Identify the Fraction

Question:
Which picture shows one half?

Options:
A. 1 out of 2 equal parts shaded
B. 1 out of 3 parts shaded
C. 1 out of 4 equal parts shaded

Correct:
A

---

#### Pattern B: Equal Parts

Question:
A shape is cut into two parts, but one part is bigger than the other. Are the parts halves?

Options:
A. Yes, because there are two parts
B. No, because the parts are not equal
C. Yes, because one part is shaded

Correct:
B

---

#### Pattern C: Compare Fractions

Question:
A chapati and another same-size chapati are used. One child gets 1/2. Another child gets 1/4. Who gets more?

Options:
A. The child with 1/2
B. The child with 1/4
C. They get the same

Correct:
A

---

#### Pattern D: Real-Life Sharing

Question:
One mandazi is shared equally among 4 children. What fraction does each child get?

Options:
A. 1/2
B. 1/4
C. 1 whole

Correct:
B

### 15. Repair Strategy for Existing Fractions Lessons

If a lesson has duplicate content:

* Rebuild the full journey from the lesson blueprint.

If a lesson has placeholder Learn:

* Regenerate Learn using the concept focus and misconception list.

If a lesson has empty Example:

* Generate one worked example with steps and answer.

If a lesson has weak Practice:

* Generate 3 tasks using the progression: concrete → visual → application.

If a lesson has invalid Quick Check:

* Generate a new MCQ using one misconception.

If a lesson has generic Reflection:

* Generate one concept-specific reflection prompt.

If a lesson is a meta-lesson, such as Practice and Application or Assessment and Reflection:

* Do not use generic title-based text.
* Treat it as consolidation or assessment.
* Use mixed tasks from the entire unit.
