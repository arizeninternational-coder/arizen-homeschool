## 5. Fractions

### What the child should understand
- A **whole** is one complete thing (one chapati, one cake, one paper)
- A **half** (1/2) means one of two **equal** parts
- A **quarter** (1/4) means one of four **equal** parts
- The parts must be **equal** — if they're not equal, they're not fractions
- We can show fractions by folding, cutting, or shading shapes
- We can see fractions in real life: sharing food, folding paper, cutting fruit

### Grade 2 vocabulary
- whole
- half / halves
- quarter / quarters
- equal parts
- fair share
- fraction
- divide / split
- shade / shaded
- NOT allowed in Grade 2: numerator, denominator, equivalent, improper, mixed number

### Prerequisite knowledge
- Understanding "equal" and "same size"
- Basic shapes: rectangle, circle, square
- Counting to 4
- Concept of "whole" vs "part"

### Approved concrete examples
1. **Chapati cut into 2 equal parts** — each part is one half (1/2)
2. **Cake cut into 4 equal parts** — each part is one quarter (1/4)
3. **Orange shared equally between 2 children** — each gets one half
4. **Paper folded into 2 equal parts** — each part is one half
5. **Paper folded into 4 equal parts** — each part is one quarter
6. **Pizza cut into 4 equal slices** — each slice is one quarter
7. **Chocolate bar divided into 4 equal pieces** — each piece is one quarter
8. **Circle divided into 2 equal parts, one shaded** — shows 1/2
9. **Rectangle divided into 4 equal parts, one shaded** — shows 1/4

### Visual/manipulative ideas
- Paper folding (halves, quarters)
- Cutting real or pretend food (chapati, cake, pizza, chocolate)
- Shading parts of shapes (circle, rectangle, square)
- Using counters or bottle caps to show equal groups
- Drawing lines to divide shapes into equal parts
- Comparing equal vs unequal parts (misconception teaching)

### Worked example pattern
1. Show the whole (e.g., one circle)
2. Divide into equal parts (e.g., cut into 2)
3. Count the parts (e.g., 2 equal parts)
4. Name the fraction (e.g., each part is 1/2)
5. Shade one part to show the fraction
6. Say: "One out of two equal parts is one half"

### Guided practice pattern
1. Show a shape divided into equal parts
2. Ask: "How many equal parts are there?"
3. Ask: "What fraction is one part?"
4. Child selects or types the answer
5. Provide immediate feedback
6. Repeat with different shapes and divisions

### Independent practice pattern
1. Show a shape with some parts shaded
2. Ask: "What fraction is shaded?"
3. Child selects from 4 options
4. Provide feedback
5. Include at least one "equal vs unequal" question

### Quick Check templates

#### QC Template H1: Identify Halves (Shape)
**Question:** "This circle is divided into 2 equal parts. One part is shaded. What fraction is shaded?"
**Options:** ["1/2", "1/4", "1/3", "2/2"]
**Correct answer:** "1/2" (index 0)
**Distractor logic:**
- 1/4: confuses halves with quarters
- 1/3: random wrong fraction
- 2/2: thinks "2 parts" means 2/2 instead of 1/2
**Feedback correct:** "Yes! One out of two equal parts is one half. Well done!"
**Feedback incorrect:** "Look again. The shape is divided into 2 equal parts. One part is shaded. That's one half."

#### QC Template H2: Identify Halves (Real Object)
**Question:** "A chapati is cut into 2 equal pieces. You get 1 piece. What fraction of the chapati do you have?"
**Options:** ["1/4", "1/2", "1 whole", "2/2"]
**Correct answer:** "1/2" (index 1)
**Distractor logic:**
- 1/4: confuses with quarters
- 1 whole: thinks one piece means the whole thing
- 2/2: confuses number of pieces with fraction
**Feedback correct:** "Correct! One piece out of 2 equal pieces is one half. Good thinking!"
**Feedback incorrect:** "The chapati was cut into 2 equal pieces. You have 1 of those pieces. That's one half."

#### QC Template Q1: Identify Quarters (Shape)
**Question:** "This rectangle is divided into 4 equal parts. One part is shaded. What fraction is shaded?"
**Options:** ["1/2", "1/3", "1/4", "4/4"]
**Correct answer:** "1/4" (index 2)
**Distractor logic:**
- 1/2: confuses quarters with halves
- 1/3: random wrong fraction
- 4/4: thinks "4 parts" means 4/4 instead of 1/4
**Feedback correct:** "Yes! One out of four equal parts is one quarter. Excellent!"
**Feedback incorrect:** "Count the equal parts. There are 4. One part is shaded. That's one quarter."

#### QC Template Q2: Identify Quarters (Real Object)
**Question:** "A cake is cut into 4 equal slices. You eat 1 slice. What fraction of the cake did you eat?"
**Options:** ["1/2", "1/4", "1/3", "1 whole"]
**Correct answer:** "1/4" (index 1)
**Distractor logic:**
- 1/2: confuses quarters with halves
- 1/3: random wrong fraction
- 1 whole: thinks eating a slice means eating the whole cake
**Feedback correct:** "Correct! One slice out of 4 equal slices is one quarter. Well done!"
**Feedback incorrect:** "The cake was cut into 4 equal slices. You ate 1 slice. That's one quarter of the cake."

#### QC Template E1: Equal vs Unequal Parts
**Question:** "Which shape shows equal parts?"
**Options:** [Shape A: rectangle divided into 2 equal parts, Shape B: rectangle divided into 2 unequal parts, Shape C: circle divided into 3 unequal parts, Shape D: square divided into 4 unequal parts]
**Correct answer:** Shape A (index 0)
**Distractor logic:**
- All other shapes show unequal parts
- Tests the core concept: fractions require EQUAL parts
**Feedback correct:** "Yes! Only Shape A has equal parts. Fractions need equal parts!"
**Feedback incorrect:** "Remember: fractions need parts that are the same size. Look for the shape where all parts are equal."

#### QC Template F1: Fair Sharing
**Question:** "2 children want to share a chapati fairly. What fraction does each child get?"
**Options:** ["1/4", "1/3", "1/2", "1 whole"]
**Correct answer:** "1/2" (index 2)
**Distractor logic:**
- 1/4: confuses 2 people with 4 parts
- 1/3: random wrong fraction
- 1 whole: thinks each person gets the whole chapati
**Feedback correct:** "Correct! When 2 children share fairly, each gets one half. That's fair sharing!"
**Feedback incorrect:** "If 2 children share one chapati fairly, we cut it into 2 equal parts. Each child gets 1 out of 2 parts. That's one half."

### Correct answer rules
- **Only 1/2 and 1/4 are valid Grade 2 fractions**
- **Parts MUST be equal** — unequal parts are not fractions
- **The answer is always "one half" or "one quarter"** — not "2 halves" or "3 quarters"
- **No fraction arithmetic** — never ask "what is 1/2 + 1/4"
- **No comparing which fraction is bigger** — not in Grade 2 scope
- **No equivalent fractions** — not in Grade 2 scope

### Distractor/wrong answer rules
- **Include 1/4 as distractor for halves questions** (and vice versa)
- **Include "1 whole" as distractor** when the child might confuse "one part" with "the whole thing"
- **Include the number of parts as denominator** (e.g., "2/2" for halves, "4/4" for quarters)
- **Include unequal parts shapes** in equal vs unequal questions
- **Never include fractions beyond Grade 2** (no 1/3, 1/5, 1/8, etc.)
- **Never include fraction arithmetic** as a distractor

### Common mistakes to avoid
1. **Unequal parts presented as fractions** — "This shape is cut into 2 parts, so each part is 1/2" (WRONG if parts are unequal)
2. **Counting parts instead of naming fractions** — "There are 4 parts" instead of "Each part is 1/4"
3. **Confusing halves and quarters** — thinking 1/4 is bigger than 1/2 because 4 > 2
4. **Thinking "one part" means "whole thing"** — "I have 1 piece so I have the whole thing"
5. **Fraction arithmetic** — adding or subtracting fractions (beyond Grade 2)
6. **Using fraction notation the child can't read** — always pair "1/2" with "one half"

### Grade 2 difficulty limits
- **Allowed:** halves (1/2), quarters (1/4)
- **NOT allowed:** thirds (1/3), fifths (1/5), eighths (1/8)
- **NOT allowed:** fraction arithmetic (1/2 + 1/4)
- **NOT allowed:** equivalent fractions (1/2 = 2/4)
- **NOT allowed:** comparing fractions (which is bigger: 1/2 or 1/4?)
- **NOT allowed:** improper fractions (3/2, 5/4)
- **NOT allowed:** mixed numbers (1 1/2)
- **NOT allowed:** decimals or percentages
- **Maximum complexity:** "One out of two equal parts is one half"

### Video guidance
- **Video is OPTIONAL for Fractions** — SVG/diagrams are more important
- **If video is used, it should be in Step 6 (Example) only**
- **The lesson must work without video**
- **Approved video should show:** paper folding, cutting food, shading shapes
- **Video should NOT be the primary teaching method**
- **Current status:** TODO: Victor to approve video URLs
- **Recommended:** Short (under 3 min), shows real objects being divided equally

### Notes for journey generator
- **Fractions journeys MUST prioritize SVG over video**
- **Every Fractions journey must include at least one "equal vs unequal" teaching moment**
- **Use real-life objects in examples:** chapati, cake, pizza, chocolate, paper
- **Always show the whole first, then divide**
- **Always emphasize: "equal parts"**
- **Never use fraction notation without also saying the word** ("1/2" AND "one half")
- **Include at least one real-life sharing scenario** (fair sharing between children)
- **Practice tasks should include shading shapes and identifying fractions from shaded shapes**
- **Quick Check must test the specific lesson concept** (halves OR quarters, not both in one QC)
